# UK Late Payment & Credit Control Kit

<aside>
🚧

**v0.9 internal build · Quality review.** Not published or sold. General information based on UK government guidance, not legal advice.

</aside>

## Start here (15 minutes)

1. **Add your customers** to Customers, with payment terms, credit limit and customer type.
2. **Add every unpaid invoice** to Invoices, with the amount, invoice date and agreed due date. Link each one to its customer.
3. **Tick Claim Statutory Interest** on business-to-business invoices where your contract doesn't set its own late-payment interest.
4. **Enter the reference rate.** The Rate Check column tells you which Bank of England base rate to look up (the rate on 31 Dec or on 30 Jun). The kit never assumes a rate.
5. **Work the Next Action column** every week and log each contact in Chase Log.
6. Delete the SAMPLE and QA-TEST rows when you're ready.

## Weekly 20-minute routine

- [ ]  Monday: sort Invoices by Days Overdue and work every Next Action.
- [ ]  Send pre-due reminders for anything due in the next 3 days.
- [ ]  Call every customer at stage 2 or later. Log the outcome and any promised date.
- [ ]  Check Customers → Credit Check and decide whether to pause work for accounts over their limit.
- [ ]  Friday: chase broken payment promises.

## How the stages work

| Days overdue | Next action | Template |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Due within 3 days | Pre-due reminder | 0 |
| 1–7 | Friendly reminder | 1 |
| 8–14 | Firm reminder and call | 2 |
| 15–30 | Final notice | 3 |
| 31–45 | Claim interest and compensation | 4 |
| 46+ | Letter before action or escalate | 5 |

These are the kit's suggested timings, not legal deadlines. Change them to suit your customers.

## What the numbers mean

- **Statutory interest:** 8% a year plus the Bank of England reference rate, simple interest, for each day overdue.
- **Fixed compensation:** £40 for debts up to £999.99, £70 for £1,000–£9,999.99 and £100 for £10,000 or more, once per invoice.
- These apply to business-to-business debts, not consumer sales. If your contract sets its own late-payment interest, use that instead.
- Check unusual cases (part payments, debts spanning several rate periods) with the Small Business Commissioner's interest calculator.

# Chasing Letter & Email Templates

<aside>
⚖️

Copy a template, fill the [brackets] from the Invoices database and send it from your own email or letterhead. These are general templates, not legal advice. Have a solicitor check Templates 4 and 5 before you first use them.

</aside>

## Before you send anything

- Check the invoice is correct and was received by the right person.
- Check the Status. Never chase a disputed amount as if it were simply late; ask for the undisputed part instead.
- Keep it factual and polite. Only mention a next step you genuinely intend to take.
- Log every contact in **Chase Log**, with the outcome and any promised date.

## 0 · Pre-due reminder (3 days before the due date)

**Subject:** Invoice [Invoice number] due on [Due date]

Hi [Contact name],

A quick heads-up that invoice [Invoice number] for £[Amount] is due on [Due date]. You can pay by bank transfer to [Bank details] or online at [payment link].

If anything on the invoice needs correcting, just let me know now so it doesn't hold up payment.

Thanks,
[Your name]

## 1 · Friendly reminder (1–7 days overdue)

**Subject:** Invoice [Invoice number] is now overdue

Hi [Contact name],

Our records show that invoice [Invoice number] for £[Amount], due on [Due date], is still unpaid. It may simply have been missed, so I've attached a copy.

Could you let me know when payment will be made? Our bank details are [Bank details].

If you've already paid, please send me the remittance details and I'll update our records.

Thanks,
[Your name]

## 2 · Firm reminder and call (8–14 days overdue)

**Subject:** Second reminder: invoice [Invoice number], £[Amount], [Days overdue] days overdue

Hi [Contact name],

I haven't yet received payment of invoice [Invoice number] for £[Amount]. It was due on [Due date] and is now [Days overdue] days overdue.

Please arrange payment within 7 days, by [date]. If there's a problem with the invoice or with timing, call me on [Phone] and we'll sort it out.

Thanks,
[Your name]

### Phone script

1. Confirm you're speaking to the person who approves or pays invoices.
2. "I'm calling about invoice [Invoice number] for £[Amount], which was due on [Due date]. Has it been approved for payment?"
3. If yes: "When will it be paid, and how?" Get a specific date and amount.
4. Confirm it back: "So that's £[Amount] by [date]. I'll email you to confirm."
5. If there's a problem: "Which part is in question? Can the rest be paid now?"
6. Log the call in Chase Log and set Promised Payment Date on the invoice.

## 3 · Final notice (15–30 days overdue)

**Subject:** Final notice: invoice [Invoice number], £[Amount]

Dear [Contact name],

Invoice [Invoice number] for £[Amount], due on [Due date], is still unpaid despite our reminders of [dates].

Please pay the full balance by [date, 7 days from today]. If we don't receive payment by then, we intend to claim statutory interest and fixed compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 [and to pause further work on your account].

If you believe the invoice is wrong, please tell me in writing by [date].

Yours sincerely,
[Your name], [Company]

Have a solicitor review this letter before you first use it.

# How the Numbers Work

<aside>
ℹ️

The Invoices database does these sums for you. This page explains them so you can check any figure before you send it. General information based on official UK guidance, not legal advice.

</aside>

## 1. When a payment becomes late

- If you agreed a payment date, the payment is late once that date has passed. Agreed terms must usually be within 30 days for public authorities or 60 days for business transactions. A longer period between businesses must be fair to both.
- If you didn't agree a date, the payment is late 30 days after the customer receives the invoice (see [GOV.UK](http://GOV.UK) for the full rule).

## 2. Statutory interest

- Rate: **8% a year plus the Bank of England reference rate**, charged as simple interest.
- The kit's formula: Amount × (8 + reference rate) ÷ 100 × days overdue ÷ 365.
- [GOV.UK](http://GOV.UK)'s worked example: on a £1,000 debt with a 0.5% base rate, a year's statutory interest is £85 (1,000 × 0.085). The kit reproduces this exactly (test row QA-TEST-01).

## 3. Which reference rate to use

- The Small Business Commissioner's guidance: the base rate in force on **31 December** applies from 1 January to 30 June, and the rate in force on **30 June** applies from 1 July to 31 December.
- Enter the rate in **Reference Rate (%)** on each invoice. Until you do, the **Rate Check** column warns you and interest is shown at 8% only.
- If a debt runs across more than one six-month period, check the figure with the Small Business Commissioner's interest calculator.
- The kit never assumes a rate. The Bank of England publishes its Bank Rate history.

## 4. Fixed compensation

| Amount of the debt | Fixed sum |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £999.99 | £40 |
| £1,000 to £9,999.99 | £70 |
| £10,000 or more | £100 |
- You can charge it once for each payment, on top of interest.
- You can also claim reasonable costs of recovering the debt beyond this. Take advice before you do.

## 5. When the kit doesn't calculate

- **Claim Statutory Interest unticked:** consumer sales, contracts that set their own late-payment interest, or invoices where you've decided not to claim. Interest and the fixed sum show £0.
- **Disputed invoices** still show figures, but Next Action tells you to resolve the dispute first.
- **Not handled in v0.9:** part payments, debts spanning several rate periods and contract-specific interest rates. Use the Small Business Commissioner's calculator for these.

## Official sources

- [Small Business Commissioner: interest calculator guidance](https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/help-and-guidance/interest-calculator/interest-calculator-guidance/)
- [GOV.UK: late commercial payments (full guide)](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/print)
- [GOV.UK: claim debt recovery costs](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/claim-debt-recovery-costs)
- [legislation.gov.uk: Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, section 5A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/20/section/5A/2002-08-07?view=plain)
