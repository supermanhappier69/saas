# Credit Control SOP

<aside>
🎯

**Goal:** get paid on time without damaging good customer relationships. **Owner:** [credit controller]. **Approver for account stops, payment plans and escalation:** [director]. **Time:** about 20 minutes a week.

</aside>

## 1. Before work starts (new customer)

- [ ]  Record the customer's **legal name** and registered address (check Companies House for companies and LLPs).
- [ ]  Get the name and email of the person who **approves and pays invoices**, and ask about their PO or approval process.
- [ ]  Agree payment terms **in writing** before work starts (use the Payment Terms & Credit Policy Template).
- [ ]  Set a **credit limit** and **risk rating** in Customers. Consider a deposit for new or high-risk customers.
- [ ]  Set **Customer Type**: the statutory interest rules apply to business-to-business debts, and debts owed by sole traders follow the Pre-Action Protocol if they ever go to court.

## 2. When you invoice

- [ ]  Invoice within 24 hours of delivery or of the agreed milestone.
- [ ]  Include the PO number, the due date in full, bank details and your late-payment terms.
- [ ]  Send it to the accounts contact, not just your day-to-day contact.
- [ ]  For invoices over £[threshold], confirm receipt and approval within 3 working days.
- [ ]  Add the invoice to **Invoices** and link the customer.

## 3. Weekly routine (every Monday)

1. Open Invoices and sort by **Days Overdue**, highest first.
2. Work each **Next Action**, using the matching template.
3. Send pre-due reminders for anything due in the next 3 days.
4. Call every customer at stage 2 or later. A call resolves more than a fourth email.
5. Log every contact in **Chase Log**, and set **Promised Payment Date** when you get one.
6. Check **Customers → Credit Check**. Raise accounts that are over their limit with the approver.
7. On Friday, chase any promise that was missed.

## 4. Escalation ladder

| Days overdue | Action | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Due within 3 days | Pre-due reminder (Template 0) | Credit controller |
| 1–7 | Friendly reminder (Template 1) | Credit controller |
| 8–14 | Firm reminder and call (Template 2) | Credit controller |
| 15–30 | Final notice (Template 3); consider pausing work | Approver decides on pausing |
| 31–45 | Interest and compensation claim (Template 4) | Approver signs off |
| 46+ | Letter before action (Template 5), then formal recovery | Approver, with advice |

These timings are the kit's defaults, not legal deadlines. Adjust them to your terms and customers, and apply them consistently.

## 5. Disputes

- Set the invoice **Status** to Disputed and stop chasing the disputed amount.
- Record exactly what is disputed and ask for the **undisputed part** to be paid now.
- Aim to resolve it within 5 working days: credit note, corrected invoice or evidence of delivery.
- Once resolved, set Status back to Open and restart at the stage that matches Days Overdue.

## 6. Payment plans

- Agree plans in writing, with dated instalments and the total.
- Decide (and record) whether you will waive interest if the plan is kept.
- Set Status to **On payment plan** and log each instalment in Chase Log.
- If an instalment is missed, go straight back to the stage that matches Days Overdue.

## 7. Claiming interest and compensation

- Claiming is a legal right for qualifying business debts, not an obligation. Decide your policy in advance and apply it consistently.
- Weigh the relationship first: an unexpected claim for small, slightly late amounts can damage trust and reputation.
- Use the Invoices figures: interest at 8% plus the reference rate, plus the fixed sum of £40, £70 or £100.
- Recovery costs above the fixed sum are only claimable if they are reasonable. Take advice before claiming them.

## 8. Account on stop

Pause new work or orders when an account is over its credit limit **or** has an invoice more than [30] days overdue, unless the approver agrees otherwise. Tell the customer in advance and in writing, and set Account Status to On stop.

## 9. Formal recovery

Before court action, a debt recovery agency or a solicitor's letter, get advice. Check whether the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims applies (debtors who are individuals or sole traders). The Small Business Commissioner publishes free guidance and an interest calculator.

## 10. Monthly review (first Monday of the month)

- Total overdue (£) and the top 5 overdue customers
- Share of invoices paid by their due date
- Broken promises and open disputes
- Customers to move to deposits, shorter terms or a lower credit limit

## Sources

- [GOV.UK: Late commercial payments](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery)
- [Small Business Commissioner: interest calculator](https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/help-and-guidance/interest-calculator/)
- [Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims](https://www.justice.gov.uk/documents/debt-pap.pdf)

# Payment Terms & Credit Policy Template

<aside>
✍️

Fill in the [brackets], delete what doesn't apply and have a solicitor review the customer-facing terms before you use them. Share your payment terms with customers **before** work starts. This template is general information, not legal advice, and is written for business-to-business sales.

</aside>

## What the law sets ([GOV.UK](http://GOV.UK))

- If you agree a payment date, it must usually be within **30 days** for public authorities or **60 days** for business transactions. A longer period between businesses must be fair to both.
- If no payment date is agreed, the payment is late **30 days** after the customer receives the invoice (see [GOV.UK](http://GOV.UK) for the full rule).
- On late business-to-business payments you can claim **statutory interest** of 8% plus the Bank of England reference rate, and a **fixed sum** of £40, £70 or £100 depending on the size of the debt.
- Consumer sales follow different rules.

## Part A · Internal credit policy

**1. Purpose.** This policy sets how [Company] gives credit to business customers and recovers overdue amounts.

**2. Who gets credit.** New customers get credit after [checks: Companies House, references, credit check]. Until then we ask for [a 50% deposit / payment in advance].

**3. Credit limits.** Standard limit £[amount]. Higher limits need approval from [role]. Limits are reviewed every [6] months.

**4. Standard terms.** Payment due [30] days from the invoice date. Different terms need [role]'s approval and must be confirmed in writing.

**5. Chasing.** We follow the Credit Control SOP and its escalation ladder.

**6. Interest and compensation.** We [will / may] claim statutory interest and fixed compensation on overdue business invoices. Exceptions need [role]'s approval and are recorded in the Chase Log.

**7. Account stops.** We pause new work when an account is over its limit or has an invoice more than [30] days overdue, unless [role] agrees otherwise.

**8. Write-offs.** Debts can only be written off by [role], after [steps], and are recorded with a reason.

**9. Review.** This policy is reviewed every [12] months by [role].

## Part B · Customer-facing payment terms

<aside>
📎

Add these to your quotes, proposals or terms of business once your solicitor has reviewed them.

</aside>

1. **Payment.** Invoices are payable within [30] days of the invoice date by bank transfer to the account shown on the invoice.
2. **Deposits.** For new customers or orders over £[amount], we may ask for a deposit of [x]% before work starts.
3. **Queries.** Please raise any query about an invoice within [7] days of receiving it. Undisputed amounts remain payable on the due date.
4. **Late payment.** If payment is late, we may claim interest and fixed compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, plus reasonable recovery costs.
5. **Suspension.** We may pause work or deliveries while any invoice is overdue, after giving you notice in writing.
6. **Credit limit.** We may set a credit limit for your account and ask for payment before further work if it is exceeded.
7. **[Optional, ask your solicitor] Ownership of goods.** Goods remain our property until they are paid for in full.

## Part C · Wording for invoices

> Payment due by [Due date] ([30] days from the invoice date). Please quote [Invoice number] with your payment. Late payments may incur interest and compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998.
>

## Customising a master template for a new industry

1. Duplicate the master and rename the entities (for example Customer → Client, Invoice → Job).
2. Swap the rule library or thresholds for the industry.
3. Replace the sample data with realistic fictional examples.
4. Re-run the QA-TEST rows and add industry-specific tests.
5. Update the official sources page and the disclaimer.
6. Bump the version and log the change.

## Version and quality standards

- **Versions:** v0.x internal builds · v1.0 first launch candidate · v1.x improvements · v2 major change.
- Record each version in the pipeline, in Development Tasks and on the product's QA page.
- Every calculation has at least one QA-TEST row whose expected value comes from an official example or a hand calculation.
- Every legal or tax statement links to an official source and is reviewed before launch.
- Nothing is marked LIVE until it has actually been published with founder approval.

## Templates

- **Commercialisation pack:** positioning · ideal customer · pricing · sales page · descriptions · demo script · marketing content · FAQ · presentation outline · outreach drafts · launch plan · distribution.
