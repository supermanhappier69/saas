# PaidOnTime marketing plan (zero budget)

*Founder working document, v1.0, 11 November 2026. Built for 8–10 hours a week and £0 of ad spend. Everything here is measurable in the founder console.*

## 1. Message

**Headline:** Get paid on time, and know exactly what you're owed when you're not.

**The three things we say everywhere:**
1. You're owed more than the invoice. UK law adds 8% plus the base rate and £40–£100 per invoice. Most owners never claim it.
2. Chasing works when it's a routine, not a mood. PaidOnTime tells you what to send today.
3. One price. No subscription. Nothing to integrate.

**The 2027 angle:** the Commercial Payments Bill makes statutory interest mandatory and non-excludable. "From 2027 your customers can't contract out of paying you interest. Here's how to claim it." Use it in every post until it stops being news.

**Tone:** plain English, UK spelling, specific numbers, no hype. We sound like a good bookkeeper, not a growth hacker.

## 3. Channels, in order of expected return

### 3.1 Free tools that rank (compounding, starts slow)

Pages that already exist and need nothing but links: `/calculator` (late payment interest calculator UK), `/free/letter-before-action`, `/free/payment-reminder-templates`, `/free/payment-terms`.

- Searches to own: "late payment interest calculator", "statutory interest calculator UK", "letter before action template", "payment reminder email template", "overdue invoice email", "how much interest can I charge on a late invoice", "late payment compensation £40".
- Each free page ends with the same offer: "Knowing the figure is the easy part" → demo or waitlist.
- Write one 800-word guide a fortnight on the questions people actually ask (the FAQ on the landing page is the seed list). Link the calculator from every guide.
- Get 20 links in the first 90 days: trade association resource pages, accountant blogs ("tools we recommend"), UK business forums, your own LinkedIn.

### 3.2 Accountants and bookkeepers (the channel that scales)

One bookkeeper chases invoices for 5–30 clients. They are easy to find (ICB, AAT, FreeAgent and Xero partner directories, local Facebook groups) and they recommend tools all day.

- Offer: a free Complete licence for their own practice, plus 30% of every sale from clients who use their referral link, for life. Track with `?ref=code` and the lead's company field until a proper referral table is worth building.
- Outreach: 10 a week by email or LinkedIn. Script: *"I built a small tool that works out statutory interest and writes the chasing letters for small businesses. Would you look at it for 10 minutes and tell me what's wrong with it? I'll give your practice a free licence either way."*
- Give them a one-page PDF to send to clients (the free templates page prints as one).

### 3.3 Founder-led outreach (fastest first customers)

- 100 businesses in the target trades (electricians, joiners, agencies, IT support, consultancies) from Google Maps and LinkedIn, in your own region first.
- Email script: *"Quick one: do you charge late payment interest on overdue invoices? Most small firms don't, and the law lets you add 8% plus the base rate and £40–£100 per invoice. I built a tool that works it out and writes the reminder. Happy to show you in 10 minutes, or just use the free calculator here: [link]."*
- Follow up once after 4 days. Expect 5–10 conversations per 100, and 1–3 customers.

### 3.4 Communities (cheap, rules apply)

- UK Business Forums, r/UKsmallbusiness and r/smallbusinessuk (read the self-promotion rules; answer questions about late payment and link only when asked), Facebook groups for trades, FSB member forums, LinkedIn groups for freelancers and agencies.
- Post useful answers, not adverts: "Here's exactly what you can claim and the wording to use" with the calculator link.

### 3.5 Launch platforms (one-off spikes)

- Product Hunt and BetaList on launch week: a day of traffic, some backlinks, a handful of sales.
- AppSumo or a lifetime-deal marketplace: hundreds of sales at a deep discount if the organic launch stalls. Decide in month 3.

### 3.6 PR and partnerships (opportunistic)

- Late payment is in the news because of the Bill. Pitch regional business press and trade magazines with a data angle: "We asked 200 tradespeople whether they charge interest" (run that survey on the waitlist form; the answers are already captured).
- Approach the Fair Payment Code / Small Business Commissioner resource pages and FSB with the free calculator.

### 3.7 Paid (not yet)

Google Ads on "late payment interest calculator" is cheap (low competition) but do not spend until trial-to-paid is known. Rule: spend only when a sale costs less than £25 at the measured conversion rate.

## 5. Launch sequence

1. **Waitlist open** (now): calculator and free pages live, waitlist form live, 3 posts a week.
2. **Private launch** (week 3): email the waitlist a founder price (Complete £69 for 7 days). Personal reply to every buyer asking what nearly stopped them.
3. **Public launch** (week 5): Product Hunt, communities, partner bookkeepers' first referrals, PR pitch with the survey data.
4. **Monthly from then:** one case study ("how X Ltd collected £3,400 of interest"), one guide, one partner batch.

## 6. Referral loop inside the product

- Reminder emails sent on the trial and Core plans carry a one-line footer, "Sent with PaidOnTime, credit control for UK small businesses", with a link. Complete removes it. Every chased invoice is a small advert to exactly the right audience (businesses that owe money also chase money).
- The customer statement and letters carry no branding; they are the customer's documents.

## 7. Measurement

- Monday: founder console (signups, trials, activation, paid, waitlist answers, emails sent), 10 minutes.
- Every lead from the waitlist gets a reply within a day with one question: "What would make you pay for this today?"
- Monthly: which channel produced the paying customers (ask at checkout: "How did you hear about us?" is a one-field addition worth making).
