# PaidOnTime business plan

*Founder working document, v3.0, 12 November 2026 (replaces v1.0 of 11 November). Every number is linked to its source or marked (assumption). Legal and market figures move, so recheck them before you quote them publicly.*

## One-page summary

**The product.** PaidOnTime is credit control software for UK businesses with 1–50 staff that invoice other businesses. It puts every unpaid invoice on a six-stage chase ladder, writes each reminder or letter with the figures filled in, and works out statutory interest and compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998. It sends nothing by itself: the user reviews and sends every message. Invoices are typed in or imported from CSV; there is no accounting integration.

**The market.** About 697,000 UK employers with 1–49 staff offer trade credit, and about 416,000 of them report a late-payment problem (derived below). Late payment costs the UK economy almost £11bn a year ([DBT and Small Business Commissioner research, July 2025](https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/late-payments-research-2/)).

**The offer.** One-off prices excluding VAT: Core £49, Complete £99, upgrade £50. A 14-day free trial of Complete, no card.

**The honest numbers.** Base case: 77 paying customers and £5.3k of revenue in year one, £41.7k over three years. Low case £2.4k, high case £366k. A £69 one-off product grows slowly. It becomes a real business only with a repeatable partner channel and a recurring tier that customers have pre-paid for.

**The bet.** About £2.4k of cash in year one (assumption) and 8–10 founder hours a week, staged by five go/no-go gates. The first real gate is day 90, 14 February 2027: continue with 10 or more paying customers, fix at 5–9, stop or pivot below 5.

**Why now.** The Commercial Payments Bill passed the House of Lords on 20 October 2026 and is now in the Commons. It is not law and has no start date, but it has put late payment in the news. Nothing in it changes the rights PaidOnTime applies today.

## The problem

The July 2025 research for the Department for Business and Trade (now the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade) and the Small Business Commissioner ([summary](https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/late-payments-research-2/), [full report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688a089a6478525675738ff9/late_payments_research_impact_on_uk_economy.pdf)) found:

- Late payment costs almost £11bn a year. Businesses are owed £26bn at any time, £17,000 per affected business. Over 1.5 million businesses (28%) are affected each year.
- An estimated 14,000 extra closures a year are attributed to it, 38 a day (a modelled figure).
- 49.6% of businesses with 10–49 staff and 26.9% of those with 0–9 were affected (Table 11).
- An affected business with 10–49 staff had £52,081 outstanding on average, spent 138 hours a year chasing and lost £2,337 of staff time. For 0–9 staff the figures are £9,214, 75 hours and £1,273 (Tables 3, 14, 15, at £16.92 an hour).
- Only 22% of businesses spent staff time chasing, 8% started legal proceedings and 7% used a debt collector (Table 12).

The gap PaidOnTime fills is a routine: knowing which message goes to whom today, and what the law lets you add. That owners rarely work out the interest is my hypothesis, not a finding (assumption). The buyer conversations test it.

## Why now

The Bill's formal name is the Commercial Payments Bill [HL]; the government has branded it the Small Business Protections Bill ([GOV.UK press release, 19 May 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/largest-crackdown-on-late-payments-in-over-25-years-as-landmark-bill-enters-parliament)). Status on [Parliament's Bill page](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/4128): introduced in the Lords on 19 May 2026, Lords third reading on 20 October, first reading in the Commons on 21 October. No Commons second reading is scheduled, Royal Assent has not been given, and the Act would start on a day set by regulations after a lead-in and transition period ([GOV.UK overview](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/commercial-payments-bill-factsheets/commercial-payments-bill-overview)). Law-firm commentary expects Royal Assent in early 2027 and effect in mid-to-late 2027 ([Gowling WLG, 16 September 2026](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-gb/insights-resources/articles/2026/the-commercial-payments-bill)), which is an unofficial expectation (assumption).

As passed by the Lords ([text](https://bills.parliament.uk/publications/67832/documents/8809)), it would: cap payment terms at 60 days, 30 for public authorities, with limited exemptions; void any contract term that excludes or varies statutory interest; give suppliers the higher of £40 or 1% of the price when a buyer raises a dispute late or without enough information; cap Small Business Commissioner fines at 1% of annual turnover; and ban construction retentions. Contracts made before commencement are unaffected. It does not change the interest formula: 8% over the reference rate under the [2002 Order](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/1675/article/4/made), plus [£40, £70 or £100](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/20/section/5A).

So the Bill is a news hook, not a dependency. Marketing sells today's rights and says plainly that the Bill is not yet law.

## Product and competition

Around that sit statements, payment plans, credit limits and account stops, a credit policy builder, a monthly review, a Monday email and calendar feed. ## Pricing and business model

- **Prices (ex VAT, one-off).** Core £49, Complete £99. Upgrading Core to Complete costs the difference, £50. Assumption: 20% of Core buyers upgrade within 90 days, adding about £6 to each new customer. That lifts £69 to about £75 and is not in the console model.
- **VAT.** [Registration is compulsory above £90,000 of taxable turnover](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register). Until then no VAT can be added, so the customer pays £49 or £99. After registration the customer pays £58.80 or £118.80 and business buyers reclaim it.
- **Stripe.** [1.5% plus 20p on standard UK cards, 2.8% plus 20p on premium cards, fees not returned on refunds, £20 per dispute](https://stripe.com/gb/pricing). Assumption: half of buyers use business cards priced as premium. That is £1.68 on a £69 sale, 2.4%.
- **Refunds.** Recommended: 30 days, no questions, on the first purchase. Business buyers have no statutory cooling-off period ([the 2013 Regulations protect individuals acting outside a business](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/4)), so this is a commercial choice. A refund costs the unreturned fee, about £1.68; a lost dispute costs £20 plus the sale. Assumption: 5% of sales are refunded.
- **Recurring, later.** Test only after 75 or more paying customers, trial-to-paid of 8% or more and activation of 40% or more. Pre-sell before building: offer Autopilot (automatic sending of stages 1–2 only, plus accounting sync) at £19 a month or £190 a year. Build only if 10 of the first 100 buyers pay. Stages 3–5 stay manual, which protects the trust position. A done-for-you service is the fast-cash fallback: Chaser's Care add-on costs about £700 a month over its base plan, so £500–£1,000 a month is a plausible price (assumption).

## Risks and mitigations

- **Nobody pays £49.** Free tools and Xero reminders may be enough. Mitigation: gates, the waitlist price question, a lower-price test.
- **Platforms catch up.** Xero is adding multi-step chasing; Paidnice, Satago and Trove cost £45–£65 a month. Sell the routine, law and documents.
- **A wrong figure harms a customer.** The app reproduces GOV.UK's worked example. Update the Bank Rate table after every decision: next 17 December 2026 ([Bank of England dates](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/september/monetary-policy-committee-dates-for-2026)).
- **Law changes.** The Bill would void contract-outs and even rename the 1998 Act. Review `lib/rates.js` and `lib/docs.js` after each stage and quarterly.
- **Legal exposure.** Templates are general information. Solicitor review before launch. Interest is not claimable where a contract has its own [substantial remedy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/20/section/8).
- **Data protection.** The app holds customers' debtor data. Act as processor, publish a privacy notice, and have a complaints procedure.
- **Email limits.** [Resend's free plan allows 100 emails a day](https://resend.com/pricing). Move to Pro (US$20 a month) at about 20 active workspaces (assumption).
- **Founder time and single point of failure.** 8–10 hours a week, weekly backups, one small server.
- **Outreach breaches PECR.** Fines are now up to £17.5m or 4% of turnover. Follow the rules in the marketing plan.
