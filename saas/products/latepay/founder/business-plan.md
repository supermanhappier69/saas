# PaidOnTime business plan

*Founder working document, v1.0, 11 November 2026. Figures marked (hypothesis) are untested. Every market statistic links to its source; check them again before you quote them publicly.*

## 1. The one-paragraph plan

PaidOnTime is a one-off-purchase credit control app for UK businesses that invoice other businesses. It tells the owner what to send to whom and when, writes the reminder or letter, and works out the statutory interest and compensation the law lets them claim. It is launched with no cash: the hosting costs under £10 a month, the first customers come from a free interest calculator and free templates that rank on Google, from accountants and bookkeepers who recommend it to clients, and from a 90-day founder-led outreach push. The one-off price proves demand cheaply; the money is then made by adding a monthly "Autopilot" tier, a done-for-you credit control service, and bundles with the other products in the pipeline.

## 2. Why now

- Late payment costs the UK economy almost **£11 billion a year**, closes **14,000 businesses a year** (38 a day) and affects **28% of businesses**. Affected businesses spend **86 hours a year** chasing, and **£26 billion** is owed at any one time, about **£17,000 per affected business** ([Department for Business and Trade / Small Business Commissioner research by London Economics, July 2025](https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/late-payments-research-2/)).
- The **Commercial Payments Bill** was introduced to Parliament in May 2026 and is expected to get Royal Assent in early 2027. It makes statutory interest at **8% above base rate mandatory and non-excludable**, caps payment terms at **60 days** (30 for the public sector), penalises buyers who dispute an invoice **less than eight days before it is due** (1% of the disputed amount or £40), and gives the Small Business Commissioner powers to fine persistent late payers ([GOV.UK overview](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/commercial-payments-bill-factsheets/commercial-payments-bill-overview), [Gowling WLG summary](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-gb/insights-resources/articles/2026/the-commercial-payments-bill)). Every small business will hear "you can now charge interest" in 2027. Almost none of them know how to work it out or what to send. That is the product.
- Credit control software (Chaser from about £199 a month, Paidnice from about £45 a month) is built for businesses with an accounts team and an accounting integration. The 1.5 million affected businesses are mostly smaller than that.

## 3. Customer

**Primary:** UK business-to-business firms with 1–50 staff that invoice on credit terms and have nobody whose job is credit control: trades and construction subcontractors, agencies, consultancies, IT and managed services, wholesalers, freelancers with business clients.

**Secondary (and the best channel):** bookkeepers, accountants and virtual assistants who chase invoices for several clients. One bookkeeper can bring ten customers.

**Not for:** consumer-facing businesses (the statutory rules don't apply), and firms already paying for integrated credit control software.

**Jobs to be done:** "Tell me what to send today so I don't have to think about it", "Make the awkward email for me", "Tell me what I'm allowed to charge and prove it", "Help me stop working for people who don't pay".

## 4. Product and positioning

For UK small businesses that chase their own invoices, PaidOnTime is the credit control system that tells you exactly what to send next and what you can claim. Unlike credit control software, it is one purchase with nothing to integrate. Unlike free calculators, it runs your whole chasing process.

What's built (v1.0): chase calendar (list, board, calendar feed, Monday email), templates 0–5 with send-and-log, interest and compensation with the Bank of England rate built in, part payments, credit limits and account stops, statements, payment plans, a credit policy builder, the SOP and guides, a monthly review, a free public calculator and free templates, a waitlist, and a founder console.

Moat: none at launch, honestly. The defensible parts are built over time: the UK-rule accuracy (rates, the Bill, the protocol), the content that ranks, the accountant channel, and the data on what gets people paid.

## 5. Pricing

| Tier | Price | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | £49 one-off (hypothesis) | Chase every invoice on time. Templates 0–3, calendar, SOP, up to 2 people. |
| Complete | £99 one-off (hypothesis) | Claim what you're owed. Interest claim and letter before action, statements, payment plans, policy builder, monthly review, up to 5 people. |
| Autopilot (v2, hypothesis) | £19–£29 a month | Automatic sending on the schedule you set, accounting software sync, multi-user. The recurring tier. |
| Credit control service (hypothesis) | £500–£1,500 a month | We chase for you, using the app. Capacity-limited, high margin, immediate cash. |
| Setup session | £149 | One hour to import invoices, set the ladder and send the first batch. |

Why one-off first: it is the cheapest possible test of whether anyone will pay at all, it undercuts the monthly tools so hard that the value is obvious, and it needs no card-on-file anxiety from the buyer. The Complete tier exists so that the people with the most money at stake (overdue invoices in the tens of thousands) pay more.

Why recurring later: one-off revenue does not compound. The plan is to prove demand with one-off sales, then launch Autopilot to the existing base in month 4–6 with a founder discount for early buyers.

## 6. Unit economics (hypotheses to replace with real numbers from the founder console)

- Blended one-off price: about £75 (60% Core, 40% Complete).
- Stripe fees about 1.5% + 20p UK cards: about £1.30 a sale.
- Hosting, email and domain: under £10 a month in total until thousands of users.
- Support: aim for under 10 minutes per customer, by making the guides and the onboarding checklist answer the questions first.
- Contribution per sale: about £73. There is no marketing spend in the plan, so payback is immediate; the cost is the founder's time.
- Target funnel (hypothesis): 3% of visitors start a trial, 15% of trials buy, so 100 visitors → 3 trials → 0.45 sales → £34. That means roughly **2,200 visitors per £1,000** of revenue at launch. The marketing plan is about getting those visitors for free and raising both conversion rates.

## 7. What it takes to reach £1 million (the honest version)

This is a product business, not a lottery ticket. £1 million of revenue is a lot of £75 sales. The founder console has a model you can play with; here is the shape of it:

| Route | What £1m needs | Realistic? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-off sales only | about 13,300 sales at £75. At 0.45 sales per 100 visitors that is about 3 million visitors. | Not on its own. Nobody sells 13,000 copies of a £75 tool without a channel. |
| One-off + Autopilot | 2,000 one-off customers (£150k) plus 1,200 Autopilot subscribers at £24 a month for 36 months (£1.04m) | Possible over 3–4 years if the accountant channel works and churn is low. This is the main plan. |
| Plus the service | 20 credit control retainers at £1,000 a month = £240k a year | Possible within 12–18 months. Capacity-limited, but the fastest cash and the best product research. |
| Plus the portfolio | The landlord compliance OS and the SOP system share the buyers and the core | Each adds a second product to sell to the same list. |

A sensible target for year one is £30–£60k of revenue with zero marketing spend; that is 400–800 sales plus a handful of retainers. If that happens, the recurring tier and the service are what make the following years worth more than the first.

## 8. Competition

| | Price | Strength | Weakness we exploit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chaser | from about £199/month | Automation, integrations, credit control teams | Price, needs integration and an accounts person |
| Paidnice | from about £45/month | Automated interest and fees via Xero/QuickBooks | Monthly, integration-only, no process or documents |
| InvoiceHelm and free calculators (SBC, GOV.UK) | free | The figure | No process, no templates, no calendar, no policy |
| Accounting software reminders (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent) | included | Automatic reminder emails | One generic reminder, no ladder, no interest, no letters, no credit control |
| Spreadsheets | free | Familiar | Nothing is automatic; nobody checks it on Monday |

Positioning rule: never compete on "automation". Compete on "the whole process, UK rules built in, one price".

## 9. Risks and how they are handled

- **Nobody pays £49.** The waitlist asks what people would pay and the console shows trial-to-paid. If it is under 5% after 200 trials, test £29 Core and a £9/month option before anything else.
- **Free calculators pull people away.** Our calculator is the top of our funnel, not the product. Every free page sells the process.
- **The Bill changes the rules.** The rates and rules live in two files (`lib/rates.js`, `lib/docs.js`) and the guidance is dated. Reviewing them is a quarterly task in the launch checklist.
- **Legal exposure.** Templates are general information, say so everywhere, and Templates 3–5 and the policy wording get a solicitor's review before launch (budget £300–£600 for a fixed-fee review, or trade it for a free licence and a case study).
- **Founder time.** The marketing plan is sized for 8–10 hours a week.

## 10. 90-day plan

**Days 1–14: ready to sell**
- Solicitor review of Templates 3–5, the policy and the terms. Decide the refund policy (recommendation: 14 days, no questions, it removes the main objection on a one-off purchase).
- Register the domain, deploy (see the launch guide), set up Stripe prices, Resend and the Monday job.
- 10 buyer conversations from your own network and LinkedIn. Script in the marketing plan. Give each a free Complete licence for feedback and a quote.

**Days 15–45: first 50 customers**
- Launch to the waitlist with a founder price (Complete at £69 for 7 days).
- Publish the free calculator and free templates, submit to Google, post in the communities listed in the marketing plan.
- Recruit 10 bookkeepers and accountants as partners (free licence for them, 30% referral on every client they bring, tracked by referral code).
- Weekly LinkedIn posts from the content calendar.

**Days 46–90: find the repeatable channel**
- Read the console every Monday: visitors, trials, paid, activation, waitlist answers.
- Double down on the one channel that produced customers. Drop the rest.
- Start the credit control service with 2–3 retainers from the trial base (people with the largest overdue totals are the warmest leads; the console can't see their data, so ask in the onboarding email).
- Decide on Autopilot: if 100+ paying customers and 10+ ask for automatic sending, build it (the core already has jobs and the mailer; it is a few weeks of work).

## 11. What the founder must decide

1. Refund policy and company details for the terms (the terms page has placeholders).
2. Prices: keep £49/£99 for the test, or start lower.
3. Whether to take the AppSumo / lifetime-deal route (fast volume at a 70% discount and a 30% commission; good for reviews, bad for brand price anchoring; recommended only if organic launch stalls).
4. Which name goes on the Companies House filing and the Stripe account.
